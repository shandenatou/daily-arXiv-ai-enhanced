TL;DR: 本文研究顺序机器人操作中的组合泛化问题，通过将泛化差距分解为边际指令偏移、指令组合偏移和上下文-动作偏移三个来源，揭示了指令空间覆盖的结构性要求。研究发现穷举元组枚举并非必要，仅覆盖完整任务空间四分之一的结构化子集即可实现强分布外性能；稀疏训练失败的主要原因是指令引导问题而非低级技能缺失，每个任务微调一个演示即可将OOD成功率从0.4%提升至54.7%。研究建议机器人数据收集应优先关注指令空间中的依赖覆盖而非穷举式任务扩展。

Motivation: 顺序机器人操作需要策略执行熟悉指令组件的新颖组合，但收集所有可能的指令元组演示在组合意义上成本过高，而稀疏覆盖的数据集在分布外重组下往往失效。作者希望通过指令空间覆盖的视角系统地诊断组合泛化失败的根源。

Method: 将组合泛化差距分解为三个来源：边际指令偏移（marginal instruction shift）、指令组合偏移（instruction-compositional shift）和上下文-动作偏移（context-action shift）。基于此分解框架诊断稀疏训练覆盖在何种条件下足够，以及训练集需要保留何种结构才能实现可靠的动作预测。同时通过对比实验分析稀疏训练失败的原因（指令引导vs低级技能缺失）。

Result: 结构性覆盖完整任务空间四分之一的子集（覆盖动作相关依赖）即可恢复强分布外性能；稀疏训练失败主要源于指令引导问题，微调每个任务仅一个演示即可将OOD成功率从0.4%提升到54.7%；对于语义依赖任务，覆盖需捕获关系结构而非仅因子多样性。

Conclusion: 高效的机器人数据收集应优先在指令空间中实现依赖覆盖，而非穷举式任务扩展。该分解框架为诊断和设计机器人操作组合泛化的数据采集策略提供了理论指导和实用建议。

Abstract: Sequential robot manipulation requires policies to execute novel combinations of familiar instruction components. However, collecting demonstrations for all possible instruction tuples is combinatorially expensive, while sparsely covered datasets often fail under out-of-distribution recombination. This paper studies compositional generalization through the lens of instruction-space coverage. We decompose the generalization gap into three sources: \textit{marginal instruction shift}, \textit{instruction-compositional shift}, and \textit{context--action shift}. This decomposition allows us to diagnose when sparse training coverage is sufficient, and what structure the training set must preserve for reliable action prediction. Our results show that exhaustive tuple enumeration is unnecessary: a structured subset, as small as one quarter of the full task space, can recover strong out-of-distribution performance when it covers action-relevant dependencies. We further find that sparse training often fails due to instruction steering rather than missing low-level skills; finetuning only one demonstration per task improves OOD success from \(0.4\%\) to \(54.7\%\). For semantically dependent tasks, effective coverage must capture relational structure rather than only factor diversity. These findings suggest that efficient robot data collection should prioritize dependency coverage in instruction space over exhaustive task expansion.
